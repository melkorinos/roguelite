Fire — Flame Shapes
Forked Flame
Flame that splits into two asymmetric tongues in the upper third. The fork reads as volatile and aggressive. Valley between tips creates unmistakable silhouette.
Leaning Flame
Windswept — base anchored bottom-left, tip reaching upper-right. Implies speed and direction. Horizontal proportions stand apart from every vertical flame variant.
Ragged Flame
Multiple irregular tips across the top — wide, organic, dancing. No single dominant point. Reads as fire burning hard and spreading. Proportions much wider than tall.

Water — Drop + Ripple Variants
Descent
Drop close to the surface — rings are tight and compressed. Tension of the moment before impact. Composition feels weighty and imminent.
Spread
Drop high up, rings spread wide below with a center impact dot. Reads as aftermath — the energy already released. Airy composition with clear negative space.
Perspective Rings
Rings tilted as if viewing the water surface at an angle. Adds 3D depth without changing the core vocabulary. The slight rotation makes the composition feel grounded.
Earth — Armor
Faceted Stone
Isometric crystal block — three visible faces, one fracture crack. Heavy, geometric, grounded. The multi-face silhouette is unlike anything else in the set.
Silhouette
Reads as a solid cube/block. Unique in the set — no other element has a hard-edge geometric silhouette.
Air — Haste
Wind Gusts
Three layered wind arcs stacking vertically, each fading in from the left. The staggered offset reads as speed and sweep. Very different silhouette from every other element.
Silhouette
Three horizontal crescent bands. Horizontal stacking is the opposite of Fire's vertical form — the two most important elements read as complete opposites.
